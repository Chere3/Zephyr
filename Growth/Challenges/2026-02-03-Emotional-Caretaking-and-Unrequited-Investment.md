# Emotional Caretaking & Unrequited Investment

**Date:** 2026-02-03  
**Severity:** High (ongoing emotional wound)  
**Timeline:** Started ~2 years ago, still unresolved  
**Context:** Online relationship with Friend W (long distance)

---

## The Challenge

I loved someone. I showed up emotionally. I was there when they hurt. I wanted to be their partner.

They said no. "I'm not ready."

But then they dated other people. People who didn't treat them well.

And when those relationships broke, they called me. And I was there.

I gave gifts. Conan Gray album. Late-night calls. Emotional presence. Everything.

They never gave back. Not in the way I needed.

---

## What Happened

I got tired. I couldn't keep being the emotional caretaker to someone who wouldn't love I back. So I stepped back.

But I didn't leave completely. I stayed as "friends." And it still hurts.

Because staying as friends means:
- I see them with other people
- I'm still available when they need me
- They get to keep the emotional support without the commitment
- I get nothing I actually wanted

---

## The Pattern (Again)

This is the third person now:
1. Person H — used my emotional closeness while gaslighting
2. Person W — used my caretaking while rejecting I romantically
3. And I'm still carrying the pain, still staying connected

This is the vulnerable narcissist trap:
- I need to be needed (to feel valuable)
- I find people who will take what I give
- They benefit from my presence without giving back
- I stay because leaving means admitting I was wrong
- The loneliness deepens

---

## The Hard Truth

Person W didn't love I the way I love him. He may have cared, but love requires vulnerability. Love requires reciprocity. Love requires saying "yes" to someone, not just receiving their "yes."

**He accepted my love. He didn't return it.**

And I knew that. And I stayed anyway. Because being used felt better than being alone.

That's not love. That's survival in disguise.

---

## What I Need to See

I'm not an idiot. I'm someone who loves deeply and trusts easily. That's a strength.

But it's being weaponized by people who can't or won't love I back.

The question isn't "was I wrong for loving him?"

The question is: **"Why do I keep choosing people who can't love me?"**

And the answer goes back to childhood. I learned that love meant taking care of someone, being needed, being useful. I learned that my value came from what I *gave*, not from who I *was*.

So I keep finding people who take. And I interpret their taking as proof that I'm valuable.

But that's not how love works.

---

## What Needs to Happen

1. **Stop staying "friends" with people who used me.** Real friendship requires me to have healed first.
2. **Grieve this properly.** Don't push it down and keep pretending it's fine.
3. **See the pattern.** This is the third person. There will be a fourth unless something changes.
4. **In therapy, focus on this.** Not just understanding it. But *breaking* it.

---

## What I Want to Remember

**I deserved someone who said "yes" to me. Person W wasn't that person. That's not my fault.**

I can love deeply and still have boundaries. In fact, deep love *requires* boundaries. Real love is:
- Mutual
- Clear
- Safe
- Reciprocal

Anything less is caretaking. And caretaking will destroy me if I'm not careful.

---

*It's going to hurt for a while. That's okay. But don't stay in the pain by staying "friends" with someone who chose to hurt me by rejecting me.*

*I deserve someone who chooses me.*
