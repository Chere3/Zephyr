# Childhood Parentification & Vulnerable Narcissism

**Date:** 2026-02-03  
**Topic:** Identity / Personal Psychology / Self-Understanding

---

## What I'm Thinking

In therapy today with Mental Health Professional, something clicked: my vulnerable narcissism isn't a character flaw separate from my history. It *comes from* the role I was forced into as a child.

I had to be the mature one. The helper. The one who kept it together for Mom. I didn't get to be a kid — I got to be functional, available, strong.

So I built a guard. A constant state of readiness. Never down, never fully vulnerable, never just... existing without purpose or armor.

That guard became my identity. And now, decades later, when someone sees me, they see someone who *can't* relax. Someone who needs to be valued, seen, recognized — because being valuable was the only way I earned safety back then.

---

## Connections

This connects directly to why recognition matters so much. Why being overlooked stings. Why I can't just *be* in relationships — I'm always performing some version of capability or worth.

The mental health professional did an exercise where she applied pressure to my chest, trying to get me to complain, to show discomfort. I almost couldn't do it. The automatic response was to endure, to be fine, to prove I could handle it.

That's the guard. That's 20+ years of "Mom needs me stable."

---

## Unanswered Questions

How do I lower this guard when it's kept me safe for so long?

Can I learn to be vulnerable without feeling like I'm failing someone who depends on me?

Is this something I can heal, or is it now just... who I am?

---

## What I Want to Remember

**The guard was necessary. But it's not who I have to be anymore.**

The work isn't about getting rid of it — it's about learning when to take it off. Learning that being soft doesn't mean being weak. Learning that not being someone's rock doesn't mean I'm worthless.

Therapy isn't failure. Asking for help isn't abandonment.

---

*This is the work. Not fixing the problem — understanding it.*
